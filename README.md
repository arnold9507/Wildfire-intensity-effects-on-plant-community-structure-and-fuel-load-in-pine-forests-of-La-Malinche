This study evaluated the effects of wildfires on woody fuel loads, plant community structure, and species diversity within pine forests of the Parque Nacional La Malinche (PNLM), Mexico. Integrating remote sensing data (dNBR index) and field sampling across burned and unburned plots. Results: Burned plots exhibited significantly higher fine (10-hour category and total fine fuels) and coarse woody fuel loads (p<0.05), with fine fuel accumulation reaching up to 3.5 times that of unburned areas due to post-fire necromass deposition. Conversely, wildfire intensity showed a significant inverse correlation with fine woody fuel load (=-0.605,p=0.048) and total surface load (=-0.633,p=0.036), reflecting fuel consumption during combustion. Rank-abundance curves demonstrated a global shift toward higher species richness and overall abundance in burned sites (F1,28=85.22,p<0.001), driven by fire-responsive species such as Ageratina mairetiana, whereas tree structural metrics (DBH and height) showed no significant differences between conditions.Conclusions: Recurrent wildfires generate positive feedback loops by increasing combustible necromass and fostering early-successional understory species without immediately altering mature canopy tree dimensions.Recommendations: Integrating high-resolution field fuel inventories with spatial satellite monitoring is recommended to develop predictive fire-risk models and design tailored forest management strategies in Neotropical mountain ecosystems.
